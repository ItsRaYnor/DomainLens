## Replace an authenticator, and sign out every other session

### Replacing an authenticator

There was no way to replace an authenticator that was lost or might be compromised: MFA had to
be turned off first, and where MFA is required that is not allowed. Account now has
**Replace authenticator**. It asks for your password and a code from the current authenticator,
or a backup code, then shows a new QR code. The old authenticator keeps working until the new
one is confirmed. After that the old secret and the old backup codes stop working, new backup
codes are shown once, and every other session of the account is signed out.

For someone who has neither the authenticator nor a backup code, an administrator can
**Reset MFA** under Admin → Users: MFA is turned off for that account and it is signed out
everywhere; where MFA is required, the user sets up a new authenticator at the next sign-in.

### A new authenticator needed proof

Starting a new MFA setup on an account that already had an authenticator asked for nothing: a
session someone else had got hold of could connect their own phone with two requests, without
the password or a code. That is now refused; replacing goes through the password and a current
code, and the proof counts for ten minutes.

### Signing out other sessions

Sessions are signed cookies and could not be ended before they expired. An account now records
when its sessions were last revoked; a session issued before that is refused on its next request.
Replacing or resetting an authenticator uses it.

### Upgrading

Nothing to do. The database gains one column on start. Sessions issued before the upgrade stay
valid until an authenticator is replaced or reset.
